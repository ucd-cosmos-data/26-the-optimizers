First half of the second week (and the previous weekend)!

### Saturday
For most of the day, everyone pursued their own activities. In the evening, we gathered with our clusters for dinner before the entire COSMOS program headed to the Rec Pool to swim, play beach volleyball, and relax by the pool.

### Sunday
Today was a free day, giving everyone the chance to spend their time however they liked. Some hung out with family and friends, others played sports or explored the area, while many caught up on laundry, relaxed, or simply enjoyed a slower pace before the week ahead.

### Monday
Today, we were introduced to supervised learning, learning how models use labeled data to make predictions and exploring the key ideas behind regression, classification, and the bias-variance tradeoff.

In lab, we first presented our mini projects before beginning our introduction to scikit-learn, a Python library we will use throughout the rest of the course. We then completed a data-splitting exercise, discussing why randomly dividing data is not always the best approach. The day concluded with a math session, where we learned the notation and mathematical foundations that will serve as the baseline for the regression methods covered in the following day's lecture.

### Tuesday
Today, we explored classification and unsupervised learning. In lecture, we compared classification to regression before learning how decision trees and ensemble methods such as bagging, boosting, and random forests improve prediction performance. We also introduced unsupervised learning, focusing on clustering and similarity measures.

In lab, we dove deeper into the mathematics behind linear regression before beginning our second mini project. Using the California Housing dataset, we applied k-means clustering to divide the state into geographically contiguous housing market regions based on location and median home value. Our task was to analyze the resulting clusters and explain both the methodology and our findings in a written report.


### Wednesday
Today, we learned about model validation, exploring evaluation metrics for supervised and unsupervised learning, as well as techniques such as train-test splits, cross-validation, and information criteria for selecting and evaluating models.

Later, we attended a distinguished lecture by David Gagnon on the science of baseball, where he discussed how statistical modeling, computer vision, and biomechanics are used to analyze player performance. We then received our datasets for the final project, with our group getting the Siena Scalp EEG dataset. The day concluded with an additional Q&A session for the our cluster with the distinguished lecturer.
